Oh, Poison Black’s latest offering, “Fix Myself”, feels less like a song and more like a DIY project gone awry—where all the instructions were read backward and upside down. The track seems hellbent on burglarizing the cool of hip hop, the peppy essence of pop punk, and the visceral angst of Rage Against the Machine. Yet, despite these lofty thefts, all it manages to deliver is a staggering cargo of absurdity. Imagine a blender that instead of mixing, insists on separating its contents back to their original states—yes, it’s that fruitless and baffling.

“Fix Myself” attempts to stitch together a Frankenstein’s monster of genres, but alas, the creature refuses to come to life. Each musical segment, possibly kidnapped from better songs, struggles under the weight of its own identity crisis, never quite meshing into anything coherent. The end result feels like a musical ‘uncanny valley’—eerily familiar yet deeply off-putting. It’s as if the band set out with a map to the treasure chest of ‘ultimate cool’ but ended up digging in all the wrong places, hitting pipelines of cringe instead.

There’s a saying that ‘everything but the kitchen sink’ can work if done right; unfortunately, Poison Black not only threw in the sink but also the entire plumbing system, and none of it connected properly. So while the intent might have been to build a masterpiece, what we got was more akin to a hastily patched up draft that desperately needs revisiting. Or maybe, just maybe, it’s so avant-garde, it’s beyond mere mortal comprehension—but let’s not hedge our bets on that.

But Oh Wait, There’s MORE! Ok, I pulled the plug at that point… and switched to another song. So you get a 2 reactions for the price of one on this episode!

Which One – Suffocated

“Suffocated” by Which One encapsulates the all-too-familiar feeling of being trapped, not just in circumstance, but in one’s own spiraling thoughts. This track strikes a chord with its stripped-back sincerity, avoiding the common pitfalls of overproduction that can dilute the raw potency of emotional music. The vocals are a standout, delivering each line with a palpable urgency that doesn’t just aim for the ears but grips the heart with a firm, almost uncomfortable pressure.

The instrumentation is cleverly arranged—sparse yet effective, creating a haunting ambiance that lingers long after the song ends. Which One uses the simplicity of the music to their advantage, allowing the lyrics room to breathe and resonate. It’s a testament to the power of minimalism in music, where less truly becomes more.

“Suffocated” doesn’t need bells and whistles to convey its message; it’s all there in the unvarnished truth of the melody and words. This song is a sharp reminder of how overpowering and isolating the struggle with inner demons can be. Which One has crafted a piece that’s both a lament and a reflection, a musical introspection that feels deeply personal yet universally understandable.

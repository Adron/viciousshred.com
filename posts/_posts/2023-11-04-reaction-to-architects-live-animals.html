“Animals” by Architects is a song that delves into the themes of disillusionment and the human condition in the modern world. The lyrics reflect a sense of frustration with the state of society, where people are often seen going through the motions of life without true purpose or connection, akin to animals following their instincts. The song criticizes the lack of authenticity in human behavior and the mindless conformity that is prevalent in contemporary culture.

The track is characterized by its heavy guitar riffs and aggressive sound, which mirror the intensity of the song’s message. The chorus emphasizes the idea that despite our advanced society, humans are still driven by basic, primal instincts. The song suggests that we are trapped in a cycle of repetitive and unfulfilling actions, much like animals unaware of their own captivity.

Overall, “Animals” is a powerful commentary on the existential crisis faced by humanity, questioning the progress we’ve made and challenging the listener to reflect on their own life and the structures of society.

Mental Correlation to Orwell’s Writings; Animal Farm

As a technologist and writer with a keen interest in the intersection of society, technology, and philosophy, I find “Animals” by Architects to be a compelling auditory canvas that vividly paints a picture reminiscent of George Orwell’s allegorical novella, “Animal Farm.” The song’s raw energy and its thematic exploration of the human condition through the metaphor of animals strike a chord similar to Orwell’s own use of farm animals to critique the socio-political dynamics of his time.

The song’s portrayal of humans as instinct-driven beings, lost in the modern world’s complexity, echoes Orwell’s depiction of the animals who, despite their initial revolution against human oppressors, end up replicating the same tyrannies they sought to escape. Architects’ “Animals” doesn’t just resonate with the narrative of “Animal Farm” but also with the broader themes of Orwell’s work, including “1984,” where human agency is eroded by oppressive societal structures.

Orwell’s writing serves as a cautionary tale about the loss of individuality and the dangers of a conformist society, themes that are powerfully echoed in the song. The line from the song, “We’re just a bunch of fucking animals,” could be seen as a modern-day reflection of Orwell’s sentiment that in the grand scheme of political power plays and societal constructs, individual human experiences and freedoms are often reduced to mere animalistic survival.

In “Animals,” Architects tap into a similar vein of disillusionment with the status quo that Orwell explored. The song’s gritty and relentless soundscapes serve as a modern backdrop to the timeless struggle between the desire for freedom and the crushing weight of societal expectations, much like Orwell’s simple yet profound prose.

The correlation between the song and Orwell’s writing on animals is not just thematic but also a reflection of the human psyche. Both challenge us to look beyond the surface, to question the narratives fed to us, and to recognize the primal instincts that can lead us to repeat history’s mistakes. In essence, the song is a raw, musical echo of Orwell’s warning from the past, a reminder that without vigilance and self-awareness, we are all susceptible to becoming the very “animals” we fear.
